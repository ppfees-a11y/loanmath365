How to evaluate a loan before you borrow

A smaller monthly payment can be easier on a budget but cost much more over the life of a loan.

Interest rate versus APR

The nominal interest rate is the rate used to calculate interest on outstanding principal. Annual percentage rate (APR) is a separate disclosure measure that can incorporate certain finance charges. Do not assume that adding a fee to interest gives an accurate APR.

Short term or long term?

With the same principal and rate, shortening the term generally raises the monthly payment and reduces total interest. Extending the term lowers the required payment but increases the number of months interest accrues. Run both terms through the comparison tool.

Fixed-rate amortization

For an ordinary fixed-rate fully amortizing installment loan, the scheduled payment stays nearly constant. Interest for each month is calculated on the unpaid balance; the rest of the payment reduces principal. Your actual lender may use daily interest accrual, different payment dates, and specific rounding.

Extra payments and fees

Extra principal payments can reduce the balance sooner. Confirm that your lender applies additional funds to principal rather than simply advancing your due date. Origination fees may be deducted from proceeds or charged separately, so distinguish the cash you receive from the amount you must repay.

Before you sign

Read the lender’s complete disclosures, confirm whether the rate is fixed, check for prepayment penalties and late fees, verify the payment date, and compare total costs. Never use a calculator estimate in place of a binding offer.
