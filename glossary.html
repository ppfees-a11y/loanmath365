Plain-English loan glossary

Definitions of common U.S. installment-loan terms and how they affect a borrowing decision.

Principal and amount financed

Principal is the outstanding amount on which interest is generally calculated. The amount financed is a disclosure term and may differ from the cash deposited into your account when charges are withheld or financed.

Nominal interest rate

The stated annual interest percentage used by a loan agreement. This site’s standard payment formula divides a nominal annual percentage by 12 to estimate monthly interest. Actual lender accrual methods may differ.

Annual percentage rate (APR)

A standardized borrowing-cost disclosure that can reflect certain finance charges as well as interest. It is not always equal to the advertised interest rate. The lender’s official APR is the reference for a regulated loan offer.

Amortization

The process of reducing a loan balance over scheduled payments. Early payments on a typical fixed-payment loan often contain more interest; later payments generally contain more principal.

Origination fee

A charge for processing or issuing a loan. It might be deducted from proceeds, financed, or collected separately. Its impact on the true cost of borrowing depends on how and when it is assessed.

Prepayment and prepayment penalty

Prepayment means paying principal earlier than scheduled. Some agreements impose fees or restrictions. A lender may also treat an extra payment as an advance on the next due date instead of reducing principal immediately.

Debt-to-income ratio (DTI)

Monthly debt obligations divided by gross monthly income, expressed as a percentage. Lenders use different definitions and underwriting standards. A calculator’s DTI is informational, not a loan qualification decision.

Fixed rate versus variable rate

A fixed rate stays constant under the stated agreement; a variable rate may change based on an index or other terms. The calculators here assume a fixed rate throughout the modeled term.

Total of payments and total interest

Total of payments generally describes scheduled payments across the loan. Total interest is the interest portion of those payments. Fees can increase the overall cost beyond principal plus scheduled interest.

Secured and unsecured loans

Secured loans use collateral that may be at risk after default; unsecured loans generally do not pledge a specific asset. Collateral, legal rights, and costs differ by product and jurisdiction.
