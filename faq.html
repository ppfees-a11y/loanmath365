Loan calculator questions answered

Straightforward answers to common questions about loan payments, APR, fees and repayment.

Why is my lender’s payment different?

Lenders may use daily rather than monthly interest accrual, round payments differently, include financed fees or insurance, or use a different first payment date. Compare the signed agreement’s amount financed, rate, payment frequency and first due date.

Does a lower monthly payment always save money?

No. A longer repayment term usually lowers the payment but can increase total interest. Compare total payments and fees, not just the monthly amount.

Is interest rate the same as APR?

Not necessarily. APR can include certain borrowing charges beyond the note interest rate. Consult the lender’s disclosures for the applicable APR.

What happens if I pay extra each month?

If the lender applies extra funds immediately to principal without a penalty, you can usually pay off earlier and reduce interest. Verify how your lender processes extra payments.

Can I use this for an auto loan?

Yes for a simple fixed-rate amortizing auto loan, but sales tax, registration, optional products, rebates and financed fees need to be included correctly in the principal or analyzed separately.

Can I use this for a mortgage?

It estimates principal-and-interest payments for a simple fixed-rate amortizing loan, but does not include property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA fees or escrow.

Can I use this for credit cards?

Not as a reliable payoff schedule. Revolving balances, minimum payments, changing purchases, fees and rates require a different model.

Does the site check my credit?

No. It is a local browser calculator. It does not check credit reports, determine eligibility or transmit loan applications.

How are fees handled?

The fee tool adds a separately paid upfront fee to borrowing cost. A fee deducted from proceeds or financed into principal affects economics differently; the lender’s APR disclosure is authoritative.

What does DTI mean?

Debt-to-income ratio compares monthly debt payments with gross monthly income. Lenders have different definitions and thresholds; this tool does not determine qualification.

Are rates on this site live?

No. You enter the rate from your own offer or hypothetical scenario. We do not claim to supply current lender quotes.

Why does the last payment differ?

The final balance can be smaller than a regular payment. A precise payoff amount depends on lender accrual, posting dates and possible fees.
