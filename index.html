How to interpret your loan results

Monthly payment is the principal-and-interest amount for a fully amortizing fixed-rate loan. The loan balance falls as payments are made. Early payments often allocate more dollars to interest because the outstanding balance is larger.

Total interest equals the sum of interest charges over the term. Upfront fees are separate unless you explicitly finance them. Total repayment is all scheduled principal-and-interest payments; compare it with any lender fees and charges.

Use the worked examples to see why the lowest payment is not always the lowest-cost loan, and the methodology to audit the formulas and assumptions.

A practical borrowing checklist

Before choosing an offer, compare the actual lender-disclosed APR, loan term, total of payments, origination fee, late charges, prepayment rules, whether the interest rate is fixed or variable, and whether the payment includes insurance or other products.

These tools assume an unsecured-style fixed-rate installment structure. They do not model revolving credit, variable-rate loans, balloon payments, deferred interest, mortgages with escrow, or lender-specific rounding policies.
