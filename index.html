6. Debt-to-income ratio (DTI)

DTI is monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. Lenders may define eligible income and debts differently.

What these numbers mean

Monthly payment is the amount due under an equal-payment schedule. Principal is the amount borrowed; interest is the borrowing charge. Total interest is scheduled payments minus principal, before separate fees. A longer term can lower the payment while increasing total interest.

The chart separates principal and interest for the first calculation. The amortization table shows how early payments generally include more interest when the remaining balance is higher.

Make a better borrowing decision

Start with the full cost, not just the advertised payment. For instance, stretching a loan from four years to six may make the monthly amount easier to manage, but it can increase the dollars spent on interest. An origination fee may reduce the funds you actually receive while leaving the repayment obligation unchanged.
