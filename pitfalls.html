Common loan comparison mistakes

Avoid errors that make a borrowing offer look less expensive than it really is.

Mistake 1: Comparing monthly payments only

A lower payment may result from stretching the loan over more months. Always compare the total amount repaid and the payoff date alongside monthly affordability.

Mistake 2: Treating the interest rate as APR

The advertised interest rate may omit fees that affect borrowing cost. Read the lender’s actual APR disclosure and understand which charges are optional or conditional.

Mistake 3: Forgetting that fees can reduce proceeds

If a lender deducts a fee from loan proceeds, you may receive less cash than the amount on which repayment is calculated. Compare actual cash received with total amounts due.

Mistake 4: Assuming extra payments always save the same amount

Savings depend on when the lender applies the payment, whether there are penalties, and how interest accrues. Confirm that extra funds are applied to principal.

Mistake 5: Relying on one calculator for every loan product

Credit cards, adjustable-rate loans, mortgages with escrow, and interest-only or balloon loans need different models. LoanMath365 focuses on fixed-rate installment math and does not calculate regulatory APR.

Mistake 6: Ignoring the impact of rounding and timing

Some lenders accrue interest daily and round each payment. A spreadsheet or monthly model may differ slightly from an official payoff quote. Ask the lender for the current payoff amount when closing a loan.

Mistake 7: Mistaking an estimate for approval

Debt-to-income and payment-budget outputs are not underwriting decisions. A lender may consider credit history, collateral, verified income, and other obligations.

A safer comparison process

Collect written offers, confirm the exact amount received, compare lender-disclosed APR and total repayment, read the fee and prepayment terms, and use the calculators to understand trade-offs—not to replace official documents.
