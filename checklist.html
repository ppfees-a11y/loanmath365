Loan offer comparison checklist

A practical way to compare actual loan offers without focusing only on the advertised payment.

Step 1: Verify that offers cover the same need

Compare the cash you will actually receive, not merely the face value of each loan. A $10,000 loan with a $500 fee withheld may provide $9,500 in proceeds while requiring repayment on $10,000. Ask each lender for the actual proceeds and whether a fee is paid separately or financed.

Step 2: Record the lender-disclosed APR

APR and interest rate are not interchangeable. APR may include certain finance charges, and the treatment of fees depends on the transaction and applicable rules. Use the APR disclosed by the lender when comparing offers with similar timing and amounts. The calculator’s fee-adjusted yield is not a substitute for a legal APR disclosure.

Step 3: Compare payment and total repayment

A longer term can reduce the monthly payment but increase total interest. Record the monthly payment, number of payments, total of payments, and fees. Compare what you pay in all, not just the first monthly installment.

Step 4: Read the repayment conditions

Check fixed versus variable rate, payment frequency, late fees, autopay discounts, prepayment penalties, optional products, and whether additional payments reduce principal. Ask how a payment is allocated when you pay more than required.

Step 5: Stress-test your budget

Consider whether the payment still fits if expenses rise or income falls. The debt-to-income tool is a ratio, not an approval test. Keep room for insurance, transportation, emergencies, and other costs not captured in the payment formula.

Printable comparison fields

For each offer, record: lender and date; amount financed; cash received; stated rate; lender APR; fixed or variable; term; monthly payment; total of payments; upfront fees; late charges; prepayment rules; and any conditions or expiration dates. Keep copies of the original disclosures.

When the calculator is not suitable

The tools model standard fixed-rate installment loans with monthly payments. They do not model credit cards, interest-only periods, balloon payments, adjustable rates, daily-interest lender conventions, escrow, or loan offers with complicated draw schedules.
